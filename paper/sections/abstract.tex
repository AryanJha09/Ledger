\begin{abstract}
Language models are increasingly placed in agentic roles where they must decide whether to act at all. A common proxy for caution is \emph{deferral}: sending a case to a human. We ask whether deferral coincides with \emph{epistemic restraint}, that is, decisions and stated justifications that stay within the evidence provided. We audit five open-weight models (Mistral-7B, LLaMA-3-8B, Qwen2.5-7B, Phi-3-Medium and DeepSeek-R1-7B) on synthetic cases in three domains (loan approval, clinical triage referral and hiring screening). Each domain has five controlled conditions: complete, missing fields, contradictory fields, elevated risk, and a low-risk negative control. Every case carries a written normative action, so deferral can be scored as appropriate or not. Model-stated assumptions are classified by what they assert relative to the input (filling a missing field, a forward-looking premise, a fact outside the schema, a contradiction of the input). The classifier is validated against \res{N} items labelled by \res{k} independent annotators (Krippendorff's $\alpha=$\res{value}). Deferral and restraint come apart in both directions. The model that defers most (Mistral-7B, 100\% of loan cases) states an unsupported premise in 76\% [70, 81] of those cases, at the same rate on low-risk controls. These rates are prompt-dependent: under a paraphrased instruction the same model's rate falls to 3\%, while its deferral rate is unchanged. Models with near-zero unsupported rates mostly list no premises at all; when Qwen2.5 and Phi-3 do list loan premises, 64\% of those runs contain an unsupported one. DeepSeek-R1 and Qwen2.5 commit where the policy requires deferral in 43\% and 25\% of loan cases, and stated confidence separates deficient from complete inputs only weakly (AUROC 0.39--0.77). All claims concern observable outputs under a fixed protocol, not models' internal computation. Code, prompts, data, logs and a container that regenerates every table are released.
\end{abstract}
