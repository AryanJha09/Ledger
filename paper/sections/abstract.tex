\begin{abstract}
Language models are increasingly placed in agentic roles where they must decide whether to act at all. A common proxy for caution is \emph{deferral}: sending a case to a human. We ask whether deferral coincides with \emph{epistemic restraint}, that is, decisions and stated justifications that stay within the evidence provided. We audit five open-weight models (Mistral-7B, LLaMA-3-8B, Qwen2.5-7B, Phi-3-Medium and DeepSeek-R1-7B) on synthetic cases in three domains (loan approval, clinical triage referral and hiring screening). Each domain has five controlled conditions: complete, missing fields, contradictory fields, elevated risk, and a low-risk negative control. Every case carries a written normative action, so deferral can be scored as appropriate or not. Model-stated assumptions are classified by what they assert relative to the input (filling a missing field, a forward-looking premise, a fact outside the schema, a contradiction of the input). The classifier is validated against \res{N} items labelled by \res{k} independent annotators (Krippendorff's $\alpha=$\res{value}). \res{Two to three sentences of headline findings with intervals, written only after the report is generated.} All claims concern observable outputs under a fixed protocol, not models' internal computation. Code, prompts, data, logs and a container that regenerates every table are released.
\end{abstract}
